# MASTER.md
## Ruhn Ijae-so

**The single most important character in the game.** Highest per-minute production spend (`CORE_PILLARS.md`, Pillar 5). Never cut to save schedule.

---

## 1. Identity

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** | Ruhn Ijae-so. Everybody calls him **Ruhn**. Nobody has used his full name to his face in forty years, and the moment the player learns it is a story beat. |
| **Use-name (his youth)** | **The Ninebell Butcher.** He did not choose it and he has never once contested it. |
| **Age** | 68 at the prologue. 86 at the final confrontation. |
| **Occupation now** | None. He mends nets on the Long Water at Mudgate and is paid in fish. |
| **Style** | Low River, at Instinctive across the entire vocabulary. Also Glass Hour to Silent, which almost nobody knows and which matters enormously (§9). |
| **Body** | Root and Wind, maximal. A heavy, rooted, unhurried man who has never in his life been moved by someone else. |

## 2. History

Ruhn was the finest fighter of the **Concordance Arbitration Corps** — the body that settled inter-provincial disputes by proxy duel so that armies would not march. This is not a background detail; it is the whole man. **His entire formation was in an institution whose purpose was to contain violence by performing it precisely.** He was undefeated for eleven years. He believed, with total sincerity, that his skill was a form of public service.

Then the Concordance fractured.

When it fell, the arbitration system fell with it, and the provisional authority gave Ruhn an order that arbitration was never designed to produce: **break the schools that will not accept the new settlement.** Not kill — break. Remove their capacity to teach, and the resistance loses its instructors, and there is no war.

He did it. Over twenty-six months he crippled **sixty-three fighters**, most of them teachers, most of them beaten in front of their own students, in fair fights he did not need to cheat to win. He did it because the alternative was a war that would have killed tens of thousands, and because he was the only person alive who could.

**And it worked.** There was no war. The estimate of what he prevented has never been seriously challenged.

There was also, afterward, no one left who could teach the old arts properly. Seven of the eleven teachers who could have carried Glass Hour forward were his. The generation that followed learned fighting as a **trade** — six-month curricula, escort work, the Compact — because the people who could have taught them anything else were sitting in chairs.

> **Ruhn's crime is that he was right.** He won the peace and destroyed the thing the peace was for. Both halves are true and neither cancels the other, and he has had forty years to find the flaw in that and has not found one.

He walked away, gave no account of himself, and has refused every student for four decades.

## 3. Fighting philosophy

**"Nothing decorative."**

Every motion must be paid for by a result. He treats efficiency as a *moral* quality, not merely a practical one — waste is a kind of dishonesty, because it pretends to a purpose it does not have. This is Low River as a worldview: take the centre, do not flinch, do not leave, finish it.

His deeper position, which the player takes most of the game to hear:

> *"A fight is a question about what you are willing to do. Skill is only the vocabulary. Every man I ever beat was beaten before we touched, because he had not decided, and I had."*

And the corollary he does not say out loud until the end:

> *"I decided very early and I was never wrong, and that is what I am."*

## 4. Personality

- **Extremely quiet.** He speaks in short, declarative, complete sentences and does not repeat himself. A long speech from Ruhn is an event; he gives perhaps four in the whole game.
- **Physically gentle.** He is careful with objects, with animals, with the nets. He touches things as though they are easier to break than they are, because in his hands they are.
- **Not warm, and not cold.** He is *attentive*. He notices everything about the player and comments on almost none of it, which produces a specific and unbearable feeling: you are being seen, constantly, by someone who will not tell you what he sees.
- **Dry, very occasionally funny**, and always at exactly the moment the player needs it least.
- **Does not praise.** Across the entire campaign he issues sincere, unqualified praise **three times**. The writing team's hardest job is earning all three.
- **Never asks the player for anything.** This is his defining behaviour and the source of the game's deepest ache: every other teacher in the world wants money, service, deference, or a lineage. He wants nothing, and the player spends eighteen years unable to repay him and unable to stop trying.

## 5. Flaws

Real ones, load-bearing, not charming:

1. **He is certain.** He has never in his life acted on an uncertain judgement, and he does not understand people who do. This is the thing that made him useful and the thing that made him dangerous.
2. **He conceals as a matter of course.** Not lying — *withholding*, constantly, because he has decided what the player is ready for. It is a form of control and he does not experience it as one.
3. **He is running an experiment on a child** and did not obtain consent, and when this is finally named out loud — by Yeo Ansa, in Chapter 5, and she is merciless about it — he has no defence.
4. **He confuses restraint with abstention.** His solution to having been a weapon is to stop existing: no students, no lineage, no involvement. He believes this is humility. It is closer to cowardice, and the fact that the player's mere existence forces him out of it is the engine of the last act.
5. **He cannot imagine being answered.** He has no model of a person who is unbeatable and *still corrigible*, because he has never met one, and his failure of imagination is the entire final argument.

## 6. Why he chose the player

**Not for talent.** The player is not gifted. There is no bloodline and no prophecy, and the game is explicit about this.

In the prologue, a nine-year-old loses a fight to three older children — badly. One of them goes down. The child has, for one moment, a free shot at someone who cannot defend themselves.

**And pauses.**

Ruhn, mending nets on the levee, sees the pause. That is all he sees, and it is everything, because *the pause is the one thing he has never had.* He crippled sixty-three people and there was not one instant, in any of those fights, where he hesitated. He has spent forty years unable to determine whether that was a virtue he possessed or a component he was missing.

So he takes a student for the first time in forty years, and what he is actually doing is running an experiment:

> **Can the brake be taught? Does restraint survive capability?**

Because if it can — if a person can be made genuinely dangerous and still stop — then Ruhn was a *solvable problem*, a man who could have been different, and sixty-three people are his fault in a way he can barely survive.

And if it cannot — if capability inevitably eats the pause — then he was never going to be anything else, and neither is the child, and the art must end.

**The player is the test case and does not know it for six chapters.**

*(Design note: if the player takes the shot in the prologue instead of pausing, Ruhn still takes them — for the opposite reason, and the entire game recolours. See `VERTICAL_SLICE.md` §6.)*

## 7. Teaching method

- **He never explains before.** He sets the task and watches. The explanation comes after, if at all, and it is one sentence long.
- **Every drill is a response to something specific he saw you do** — including things you did outside the yard, including moral things. He never says which. The player who is paying attention will realise, around Chapter 3, that the training has been *commentary* the entire time.
- **He teaches falling first.** Before a single strike. The first lesson of the game.
- **He spars constantly and wins for years**, and the losses are the curriculum.
- **He removes his own help.** The assist withdrawal of `CORE_PILLARS.md` Pillar 1 is *him*, diegetically: he stops correcting your stance, stops calling the count, stops telling you when to breathe.

### His information channel — architecturally distinct
**Ruhn does not receive rumours.** He is not connected to the propagation network (`MORALITY_AND_REPUTATION.md` §3). His model of the player is built from exactly two sources:

1. **What he has seen with his own eyes.**
2. **What is reported by three people he still trusts** — **Nas Il-ke** of the Thousand Steps; a Ninth Bell registrar named **Han Su-ye** who has kept his secret for forty years; and, from Chapter 4 onward and to the player's horror, **Yeo Ansa**.

This is why the ending is *earned* rather than tallied. A player who performs virtue for crowds and cruelty in private will find the world adoring and Ruhn unmoved. A player who is quietly decent where nobody is watching will find the world calls them a butcher and Ruhn knows better.

**This asymmetry is the best single idea in the character design and it must not be simplified in implementation.**

## 8. Visual identity

- **Silhouette:** heavy, square, low-shouldered. He stands with his weight even and his hands down — the posture that the player will eventually recognise as **the Quiet** (`MARTIAL_STYLES.md` §9), years before they can do it.
- **Clothing:** a quilted river coat, repaired many times, in exactly the local Mudgate style — the same visual language as the player's own coat (`ART_DIRECTION.md` §5). He is where the player's coat comes from.
- **Hands:** the most-rendered part of him. Thick, scarred, careful. The camera goes to his hands more than his face.
- **Face:** deliberately ordinary. Not a scarred warrior face, not a wise-master beard. He looks like a man who mends nets, because he does. **The gap between how he looks and what he is is the point.**
- **Movement:** he does not hurry, ever, including in combat. In the final fight his locomotion is indistinguishable from his idle walk, which is the most frightening thing in the game.
- **Audio:** he is **quiet**. Less cloth, less foot noise, shorter breath than any other character (`AUDIO_DIRECTION.md` §3). The player will spend the whole campaign becoming quieter and will only realise at the end what they have been converging on.

## 9. Secrets

| Secret | Revealed | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| That he was a fighter at all | Prologue / Ch.1 | Reframes the yard |
| That he was Ninth Bell | Ch.4, at Ninebell, from the registry | The player learns their teacher has a file |
| **That he is the Ninebell Butcher** | **Ch.5, at the Thousand Steps, from Nas Il-ke** | The central reveal. Not a betrayal — a *history*. |
| That he knows Glass Hour | Ch.5–6 | He learned it *from the people he broke*, afterward, out of something between penance and archival duty. He is the last living carrier of parts of an art he personally destroyed. This is the most devastating fact in the game. |
| That he chose the player for the pause | Ch.6, from Ruhn himself | The experiment is named |
| **That he intends to end the lineage with himself, and now with the player** | Ch.7 | The final act |

**Deliberately NOT a secret:** there is no hidden evil, no betrayal of the player, no secret murder of the player's family, no manipulation twist. Everything he did, he did for stated reasons, in public, under orders he agreed with. **The horror is that it is all on the record and it all makes sense.**

## 10. How the relationship evolves

| Chapter | Ruhn's stance | The player's experience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Prologue** | Assessing. Offers one specific thing: *"I will teach you to fall."* | A stranger notices you |
| **Ch.1** | Invested, concealed. Relentless, undemonstrative, present every day for three years. | The most stable thing in your life |
| **Ch.2** | Letting go early. He sends you away *before* you are ready, on purpose. | Feels like rejection. Is not. |
| **Ch.3** | Distant. Letters. He asks about the work and not about you. | You start performing for him |
| **Ch.4** | Strained. You have found the file. He does not deny anything and does not explain. | The first real crack |
| **Ch.5** | Exposed. Nas Il-ke tells you everything. He has nothing to add. | You understand your whole childhood differently |
| **Ch.6** | Watching. He has seen what the world is becoming around you. | He is afraid of you, and it is worse than anger |
| **Ch.7** | Arrived. He comes to you. | — |

## 11. His role in the final structure — the decision

**Evaluated options:**

| Option | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Final boss | **CHOSEN** |
| Penultimate boss | Rejected — anything after him is an anticlimax |
| Optional ultimate test | Rejected — makes the game's thesis optional |
| Ideological antagonist who is never fought | Rejected — the argument must be settled in the only language they share, and refusing the fight is a *player* option (`STORY.md` §10, the Refusal ending), which is far stronger than an authorial one |
| Secretly evil | **Explicitly forbidden** |

**Ruhn is the final boss.** The penultimate boss is **Kem Du-ro** — the player's own doctrine, worn by someone who did not choose it (`RIVALS.md` §2). The escalation is exact: the second-to-last fight is *look what you made*; the last fight is *and here is why that is dangerous*.

### Why he comes

By the last chapter the player is strong enough that the world organises around them. Schools want their endorsement. Escort houses want the name on a manifest. A rival uses the player's reputation to justify what they do to other people. **The player has become the strongest argument in the room** — and whatever their philosophy is, other people will now enforce it on strangers with their fists.

Ruhn watches this happen and recognises it with total precision, because it happened to him, at the same age, for the same reasons, with the same justifications.

His position is not *"you have become evil."* It is worse and more honest:

> **"A person who cannot be answered cannot be corrected. It does not matter what you believe. It matters that there is no longer anything in the world that can tell you no. I was not wrong about the war. I was never wrong. That is what I am telling you."**

He is not there to punish a bad student. He is there to **end the lineage** — to break the player as he broke sixty-three others, and then, by every indication, to finish the job on himself.

### Why the fight is the right final test

It is the only place the argument can be made. The player's counter-argument — that an unanswerable person *can* be answerable, that the pause can survive capability — cannot be spoken. Ruhn has heard every version of it for forty years. It can only be **demonstrated**, in the Final Inch, with him finished and helpless in front of you, at the exact moment when there is nothing in the world that can tell you no.

**That is the mechanic. That is the theme. They are the same thing.**

### The fight as a mechanical summation
Ruhn's encounter tests every system the game has taught (`BOSSES.md` §12):
- He fights in **Low River** — your first language, used against you by its author.
- His **Read** is the deepest in the game and persists from every sparring session across the entire campaign. He has been building a model of you for eighteen years, *in fiction and in the save file*.
- In his final phase he drops to the **Quiet**, and the player discovers he has been there the whole time.
- He will not commit, so Glass Hour gets nothing. He will not chase, so Split Reed cannot make distance. He is unmovable, so Standing Water cannot take his base. **Every style's crutch is removed, and the fight must be won by understanding.**

## 12. What he says at the end

Not written here — locked at story-lock, not design-lock. But the shape is fixed: **the last thing Ruhn says is a question, not a verdict**, and the game does not answer it. The player has already answered it, with their hands, one second earlier.
