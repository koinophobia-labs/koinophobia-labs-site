# MARTIAL_STYLES.md
## The arts of the Concord

Read `COMBAT_SYSTEM.md` §4 (the intent grammar) first. Styles are not loadouts; they are dialects of one language.

---

## 1. How many styles the game needs, and why

**Six, plus one non-style.** Not more.

The number is derived, not chosen. Every style must own a unique position in a two-axis space: **which resource it primarily attacks** (Vitality / Structure / Breath) × **which distance band it owns** (Outside / Long / Mid / Contact). Six styles fill that space with no redundancy and no gaps:

| Style | Primary target | Distance | One-line philosophy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Low River** | Structure | Mid | Take the centre and walk through the man. |
| **Split Reed** | Vitality | Long / Outside | Never be where he is aiming. |
| **Standing Water** | Structure | Contact | A fight is a negotiation about where a body may be. |
| **Glass Hour** | Structure (borrowed) | Reactive, any | Do nothing first. Add to what already moves. |
| **Hooks** | Vitality + Breath | Mid / Contact | There are no rules. Whatever works, until it doesn't. |
| **False Face** | Breath + Structure | Mid | The fight is in his head. Hit the picture, not the man. |

A seventh style would have to duplicate one of these positions, which would make it a *variant*, which would make it a loadout. That is exactly the failure Pillar 2 forbids.

**They form a web, not a ladder.** Matchups are deliberately non-transitive: Split Reed beats Low River's straight-line pressure; Low River beats Hooks' volume by taking the centre; Hooks beats Glass Hour by never committing enough to be borrowed; Glass Hour beats Split Reed's big committed kicks; Standing Water beats anyone standing in a stance; False Face beats anyone with a model of you. No style is the answer. Every style is *an* answer.

## 2. A note on naming and on fiction

The arts have two names each, which is a fact about the world (`WORLD.md` §6): the **registry name** assigned when the fallen Concordance catalogued its martial schools for licensing, and the **common name** that people actually use. "Registered as Third Form, Vessel Lineage" — everybody calls it Hooks. The gap between the two names is the gap between the institutions and the street, which is one of the game's themes.

These are fictional arts. They draw on real principles because real principles make better mechanics, not because we owe anyone accuracy. No real school is named, claimed, or represented.

## 3. LOW RIVER
*Registry: First Form, Arbitration Corps (Interior). Common: Low River, or "walking."*

**The spine art. The one Ruhn teaches. The one the player will carry for the whole game.**

- **Real-world inspirations:** linear internal power arts (Xingyi's directness), boxing structure and weight transfer, folk-wrestling entries.
- **Philosophy:** Nothing decorative. Every motion must be paid for by a result. The shortest distance between two fighters is the centre line, and the centre line belongs to whoever will not leave it. Efficiency is treated as a *moral* quality, not merely a practical one — this is Ruhn's whole worldview compressed into a way of standing.
- **Stance:** Square-ish, weight 60/40 to the front foot, hands low and close, elbows against the ribs. Unglamorous. It looks like a man about to lift something heavy.
- **Preferred distance:** Mid. It wants to be exactly where hands reach and to *stay* there.
- **Movement behaviour:** Forward. Short, flat, driving steps that never cross. It does not bounce, it does not circle, it does not retreat well.
- **Defensive philosophy:** Defence is position. You do not block a strike so much as stand where it is weak. Structure recovery is the best in the game while advancing, and the worst while retreating.
- **Offensive philosophy:** Take one quadrant and destroy it. Every technique drives through the fore quadrant until the base collapses backward.

**Signature mechanic — the Line.** Advancing directly toward the opponent's centre builds **Line**, a stacking state (3 tiers). Line grants escalating structure damage, anti-flinch on light impacts, and shortened recovery. **Retreating drops all Line instantly.** Angling holds it but does not build it.

The Line is the style arguing with you. It says: you cannot back up. Every fight in Low River is a question about whether you are willing to stay in range of harm — which is why it is the art of a man who broke sixty-three people and the art the game opens with.

- **Strengths:** best structure pressure in the game; excellent against anyone who wants to trade; superb Breath economy; forgiving frames.
- **Vulnerabilities:** angles, lateral movement, long-range kickers, anyone who can circle and re-establish distance. Terrible when forced to retreat. Predictable — its virtue is its weakness, and elite opponents read the Line immediately.
- **Signature techniques:** *Settle* (the guard — a weight-drop that converts incoming force into a shorter step), *Through-Palm* (the Drive; the style's whole thesis), *Shoulder Gate* (a Check that steals the centre), *Nail* (a low, flat kick to the lead knee that degrades a quadrant directly), *Come Down* (a Displace from the Line that plants them where they stood).
- **Mastery progression:** Line becomes buildable through *angling* at high mastery — the master of Low River can hold the centre without occupying it, which is a genuinely different style from what the child learned with the same name.
- **Good against:** Hooks, pressure brawlers, anyone who plants their feet.
- **Poor against:** Split Reed, False Face, anyone with genuine footwork.

## 4. SPLIT REED
*Registry: Ninth Form, Coastal Provinces. Common: Split Reed, or "the measure."*

- **Real-world inspirations:** long-range kicking arts, savate's precision at distance, the outside game of Muay Thai (teeps, long knees, checking).
- **Philosophy:** Distance is a weapon and the only one you own. A fighter who must ask permission to be hit is already losing. Never be where he is aiming; be a half-step further than he thinks.
- **Stance:** Bladed, side-on, lead leg light and ready to lift, hands high but relaxed and almost lazy. Deceptively casual. It looks like someone waiting for a train.
- **Preferred distance:** Long, drifting to Outside. It is the only style that is *comfortable* where nothing reaches.
- **Movement behaviour:** Constant, small, lateral and rearward. Never stands still. Does not like walls.
- **Defensive philosophy:** Do not be there. Evade distance is the longest in the game; guard is the worst. Checking incoming low attacks with the lead shin is a core defensive tool.
- **Offensive philosophy:** Damage from outside their reach, then leave. It is the only style that attacks Vitality as its primary target, and the only one that can genuinely win a fight without ever entering Mid.

**Signature mechanic — the Measure.** The style maintains a **measure**: an optimal distance band that shifts with your reach, your opponent's reach, and your current technique. Acting from inside the measure is normal. Acting **from the exact edge** grants *Clean*: the technique is faster, cheaper in Breath, and cannot be punished on whiff. The measure is communicated through camera framing and a subtle shift in the character's weight — never a UI ring.

The measure *decays* if you stand still. It must be re-taken by lateral movement. So the style is constantly, physically asking you to move, which is the philosophy made into a control habit.

- **Strengths:** the only reliable answer to pressure styles; wins against Low River; safest style in the game when played correctly; excellent against multiple opponents because it never commits to being anywhere.
- **Vulnerabilities:** the clinch destroys it; walls destroy it; a single Seize can end the fight. Low Breath efficiency on committed kicks. Bad against anyone patient enough to simply walk it down and eat two kicks to do so.
- **Signature techniques:** *Long Reed* (a probing front kick that denies entry and degrades the fore quadrant), *Cut* (a low kick to the lead leg's supporting quadrant), *Turn Away* (a Redirect-by-absence — a pivot that makes their commitment whiff by three inches), *Answer* (a rear round kick thrown into their entry — the highest single-hit Vitality damage in V1), *Reed Bends* (a retreating Check that buys the measure back).
- **Mastery progression:** the measure widens and stabilises; at Silent tier the style gains *re-measure in recovery*, meaning you can retake the edge while still finishing a kick. At Instinctive the style gains the ability to hold the measure while moving *forward*, which turns a purely defensive art into a stalking one.
- **Good against:** Low River, Standing Water, Hooks — anything that must close.
- **Poor against:** Glass Hour (borrows the big kicks), False Face (lies at the measure), enclosed spaces.

## 5. STANDING WATER
*Registry: Fourth Form, Iron Road Compact. Common: Standing Water, or "custody."*

- **Real-world inspirations:** jacket wrestling (judo, shuai jiao), Greco-Roman upper-body control, clinch work.
- **Philosophy:** A fight is a negotiation about where a body is allowed to be. Strikes ask; grips decide. You do not need to hurt a man you are holding.
- **Stance:** Low, wide, hands forward and open, head off-line. Looks like it is reaching for something. Unmistakable at a distance.
- **Preferred distance:** Contact. It has essentially nothing at Long, and it knows it.
- **Movement behaviour:** Direct, heavy, level-changing. Walks through the Mid band as fast as possible to get to Contact. Superb Structure but poor evasion.
- **Defensive philosophy:** Take the grip. A held opponent cannot strike well. Defence and offence are the same act.
- **Offensive philosophy:** Almost no Vitality damage until the terminal. It attacks Structure exclusively and then converts. This makes it the slowest style to *hurt* someone and the fastest to *finish* them.

**Signature mechanic — Custody.** (Full spec: `COMBAT_SYSTEM.md` §8.) Standing Water is the only style with full access to the grip graph, including the deep nodes (back control, head-and-arm, the chokes). Other styles can Seize; only Standing Water can *live* there.

Custody is where the game's moral core is sharpest. Every deep node offers the widest Final Inch menu in the game — throw, choke, break, submit, release. A Standing Water player is asked the game's central question more often, and more precisely, than anyone else.

- **Strengths:** the widest terminal options; the best answer to any style that relies on a stance (it dismantles stances by definition); excellent against armed opponents; beats Glass Hour because there is no momentum to borrow from a grip.
- **Vulnerabilities:** multiple opponents (being in Custody while a third party is standing is close to suicidal); strikers with good frames who punish the entry; anyone who simply will not engage. Very high Breath cost.
- **Signature techniques:** *Collar* (the entry Seize), *River Stone* (a hip throw that plants them in the direction their base is already broken), *Fold* (a Displace that uses their own retreat against them), *Take the Back* (node advance — the gateway to the choke terminals), *Let Go* (a deliberate release with a shove; scores as dominance without harm, and the Cut hates it).
- **Mastery progression:** node transitions shorten, escapes become harder to execute against you, and at Instinctive the style gains *chained conversions* — a throw that flows directly into a ground node without a reset, which is when it starts to look genuinely frightening.
- **Good against:** Low River, Split Reed, Glass Hour, weapon users.
- **Poor against:** Hooks (too fast, too many hands), crowds, open ground against a runner.

## 6. GLASS HOUR
*Registry: Second Form, Thousand Steps (Upper Temples). Common: Glass Hour, or "borrowing."*

**The dying art.** Fifteen years to learn, useless in a freight car, and the last generation that could teach it properly were the people Ruhn broke.

- **Real-world inspirations:** redirection and joint-lock arts, sensitivity/contact-reflex training, the counter-fighting principle of adding to an opponent's committed momentum.
- **Philosophy:** Do nothing first. A fight only has energy in it because someone chose to put it there. Take what is offered and give it back with interest. To attack first in Glass Hour is a technical error and, in the temples, a small moral failure.
- **Stance:** Upright, almost relaxed, one hand forward at chest height and slightly open, feet quiet and close. It does not look like a fighting stance. That is the point, and it is why the style reads as arrogant to everyone who is not a practitioner.
- **Preferred distance:** Any — it is defined by reaction, not by range. Strongest at Mid, functional everywhere.
- **Movement behaviour:** Minimal. Pivots, small circles, weight changes rather than steps. The least travelled ground of any style.
- **Defensive philosophy:** Defence *is* the offence, literally. The deflect is the core technique, not a tool.
- **Offensive philosophy:** Weak. Genuinely, deliberately weak. Glass Hour's unassisted offence is the worst in the game.

**Signature mechanic — the Borrow.** A successful Deflect does not merely negate: it **stores** a fraction of the incoming force as **Borrowed** charge (up to 3). Borrowed charge is spent to:
- Convert the opponent's *next* committed action into a Displace (a throw they walked into).
- Instantly break a quadrant they are leaning on.
- Extend the Final Inch window dramatically.

**The style has an anti-mechanic built in, and it is the best thing about it:** an opponent who commits to nothing gives you nothing. Against a patient counter-fighter, Glass Hour is close to helpless, and the practitioner must either learn to *provoke* — which violates the style's own philosophy — or lose. This tension is the thematic engine of the Thousand Steps faction, an art whose purity is also its extinction.

- **Strengths:** devastating against aggression; the highest ceiling in the game; the best Breath economy by far; it makes the Final Inch's rarest terminals available most often.
- **Vulnerabilities:** feints (a Lie offers nothing to borrow and eats your deflect), patience, low-commitment volume, multiple opponents, and its own offensive poverty. The hardest style to learn, by design, with the harshest failure penalty on a missed deflect.
- **Signature techniques:** *Listen* (the deflect; the first and last thing the style teaches), *Hour Turn* (spend 1 Borrowed: convert their commitment into a pivot-throw), *Glass* (spend 2: a joint-lock Break, the most surgical terminal in the game), *Give Way* (a retreating Redirect that costs nothing and repositions both fighters), *Empty Hand* (spend 3: the style's only initiating technique, and it is a provocation — a deliberate opening offered to bait a commitment. Teaching it is controversial in the temples.)
- **Mastery progression:** at Sound, the deflect window widens. At Silent, Borrowed charge persists between exchanges rather than decaying. At Instinctive, the style gains *pre-Borrow*: deflecting on the correct read grants charge even on a Lie, because a master can tell the difference. This is the single most satisfying mastery payoff in the game — the system that hard-countered you becomes fuel.
- **Good against:** Hooks, Split Reed, aggressive bosses, anyone with a temper.
- **Poor against:** False Face, patient fighters, Standing Water, crowds.

## 7. HOOKS
*Registry: Third Form, Vessel Lineage. Common: Hooks.*

**The modern art.** Twenty years old, no lineage, assembled from whatever worked, and the fastest-growing style in the Concord. The old schools despise it. It keeps beating them.

- **Real-world inspirations:** boxing volume and head movement, dirty clinch work, the pragmatic synthesis instinct of modern mixed competition.
- **Philosophy:** Purity is a luxury for people who are not being hit. There is no such thing as a rule, only a thing that has not stopped working yet. Steal from everyone. Apologise to no one.
- **Stance:** High tight guard, chin down, weight forward on the balls of the feet, constant small head movement. It is the only stance in the game that is *never still*.
- **Preferred distance:** Mid collapsing to Contact. It wants to be in your chest.
- **Movement behaviour:** Bouncing, aggressive, cutting off angles rather than circling. Excellent at closing, poor at leaving.
- **Defensive philosophy:** Head movement and volume. Do not let them start.
- **Offensive philosophy:** Overwhelm. Attack Vitality and Breath simultaneously with more actions than the opponent can process.

**Signature mechanic — Tempo.** Consecutive actions with no pause build **Tempo** (0–5). Higher Tempo means faster startup, shorter recovery, and increased Breath *drain*. Critically: **Tempo only falls. It never holds.** Stop moving for more than a beat and it bleeds away.

So Hooks is a style that will not let you think, and that punishes you for the same thing it demands. It is the most exciting style to play and the easiest to lose with. It is also, deliberately, the style a player reaches for when they are angry — and the game notices that.

- **Strengths:** the best pressure in the game; wins against technicians who need setup time; the only style that can genuinely rush down a boss's opening phase; extremely satisfying.
- **Vulnerabilities:** Breath. It gasses. A Hooks fighter who has not closed the fight by the two-minute mark is losing it. Loses to patience, to Low River taking the centre, and to Glass Hour's borrowing.
- **Signature techniques:** *Double* (the fundamental two-hand entry), *Post* (a Check with the lead hand that buys Tempo), *Hook Under* (a Seize into a dirty clinch with strikes available — Hooks' cut-down version of Custody), *Run Him Down* (a Drive that only exists at Tempo 4+), *Doesn't Matter* (a trade — you accept their hit to land yours; the only technique in the game that deliberately spends your own Vitality).
- **Mastery progression:** at high mastery Tempo decays more slowly and can be maintained through defensive actions — a master of Hooks can *deflect* without losing Tempo, which transforms it from a glass cannon into a genuinely complete art. This mirrors the faction's arc.
- **Good against:** Glass Hour (early), technicians, Standing Water.
- **Poor against:** Low River, patience, long fights, Split Reed's measure.

**Scope:** V1 candidate cut. See §10.

## 8. FALSE FACE
*Registry: unregistered. Common: False Face, Market Hands, or "lying."*

The art of the Cut. It has no registry entry because nobody ever taught it in a school; it was assembled in mining-town pits by people who could not afford to lose.

- **Real-world inspirations:** feinting and rhythm-breaking in boxing, deceptive/drunken movement principles, pit-fighting pragmatism.
- **Philosophy:** You are not fighting a man. You are fighting his *picture* of you. Change the picture and the man walks into a wall on his own.
- **Stance:** Deliberately ambiguous and inconsistent — it changes between exchanges. Loose, unbalanced-looking, hands at odd heights. It is the only style with *multiple* idle stances, and the AI's read of your stance is intentionally unreliable.
- **Preferred distance:** Mid. It lives exactly where a decision has to be made quickly.
- **Movement behaviour:** Irregular rhythm. Stops and starts. Never travels at a constant speed.
- **Defensive philosophy:** They cannot hit what they have mis-predicted.
- **Offensive philosophy:** Attack Breath and Structure by causing wrong reactions. A defended attack that was never real costs them everything and you almost nothing.

**Signature mechanic — the Lie.** False Face's every technique has a feint variant, and the style extends the universal Lie (`COMBAT_SYSTEM.md` §6.1) in three ways: Lies are nearly free in Breath, Lies can chain into each other, and a successful Lie **actively corrupts the opponent's Read table** — inserting a tendency you do not actually have.

**This is the only style in the game that attacks another system rather than another fighter.** It is why False Face is weak early (a dock tough has no model of you to poison) and elite late (the better the opponent's Read, the more there is to corrupt). An inverted power curve is unusual and correct here: the art of the desperate becomes the art that beats gods.

- **Strengths:** the best answer to elite adaptive opponents in the game; superb Breath economy; wins fights without landing much; deeply personal to play.
- **Vulnerabilities:** opponents who do not react (patient counter-fighters read as immune); low-intelligence and animal opponents (you cannot lie to something with no model — which is played for both comedy and design); poor raw damage; requires the player to understand the opponent, which is a genuine skill wall.
- **Signature techniques:** *Show* (a pure feint with no follow-through, cheapest action in the game), *Two Faces* (a Lie that chains into a second Lie), *Market Hand* (an open-hand slap that does negligible damage and enormous Structure damage to a fighter who flinched), *Sell It* (a deliberate stumble — feigning a broken quadrant to bait a finish, and one of the very few ways to bait an opponent's Final Inch and punish it), *Nothing There* (an attack that becomes a Lie *after* the opponent commits to defending it, available only at Instinctive).
- **Mastery progression:** Lies become indistinguishable from real techniques at the animation level. At Sound tier a careful opponent can still spot the difference in the wind-up; at Silent they cannot. This is a mastery progression expressed **entirely through animation fidelity**, which is the purest expression of Pillar 1 in the game.
- **Good against:** bosses, rivals, anyone smart.
- **Poor against:** patience, fools, animals, crowds.

**Scope:** V1 cut. Its core idea (the Lie) survives as a universal mechanic. See §10.

## 9. THE QUIET
*No registry. No common name. People who have seen it disagree about whether it exists.*

**The mechanical expression of becoming a Martial God.**

### Unlock
Not purchasable, not story-gated, not announced. The Quiet becomes available when the player has:
- **≥ 8 techniques at Instinctive mastery**, drawn from **≥ 3 different styles**, and
- reached **Silent** style-mastery in at least two styles, and
- experienced at least one fight in which they won without using a style's signature mechanic.

The game does not tell you. One day, holding the stance ring, there is a fourth position with nothing in it.

### What it is
In the Quiet you have **no stance bonus and no signature mechanic**. You give up the Line, the Measure, Custody's deep nodes, the Borrow, Tempo, and the Lie's corruption. Mechanically you are, on paper, *worse*.

In exchange:

1. **Your Instinctive techniques become universally available**, expressed through the intent grammar rather than through style bindings. Strike + Angle no longer produces "the Low River answer" — it produces *whichever* of your Instinctive techniques is correct for this distance, this opponent state, this quadrant. The game selects, from your own mastered vocabulary, the right sentence.

2. **Accord.** When you execute a technique that is genuinely *correct* for the situation — right distance band, right target resource for their current weakness, right quadrant — you enter **Accord** for a short window: the next technique costs no Breath and may come from any lineage, and the transition is invisible.

Chaining Accord is the highest expression of play in the game. It requires reading the opponent correctly, repeatedly, at speed. It cannot be executed by pattern. It cannot be practised into a rote string, because the "correct" technique changes with the opponent's state.

### Why this is the right answer to "what is a martial god"
A player in the Quiet who does not understand the fight is **worse than a competent stylist** — they have thrown away every crutch and gained nothing. A player who does understand it is doing something no style can do, because they are no longer answering with an art. They are answering the *question*.

This is skill, not stats. It is the thesis: mastery is the widening of choice. In the Quiet the player has the maximum number of available actions at every instant and the fewest guardrails, which is precisely what it means to be unanswerable — and precisely why Ruhn thinks it should not exist.

### Presentation
The Quiet has no stance. The character simply stands, hands down, weight even. It is the same posture Ruhn has been standing in since the first hour of the game, and the player will recognise it.

## 10. Do players choose, cross-train, or blend? — and the Body constraint

**They cross-train, but they cannot be great at everything.** The mechanism is the **Body** (`PROGRESSION.md` §2).

- Any style can be carried to **Sound** mastery by anyone. You can learn all six.
- Advancing a style to **Silent** or **Instinctive** requires a body that suits it. Bodies are shaped by conditioning across three paired attributes under a hard total budget: **Root↔Mobility**, **Density↔Speed**, **Wind↔Burst**.
  - Standing Water wants Root and Density. Split Reed wants Mobility and Speed. These are opposite ends of the same sliders.
  - Low River wants Root and Wind. Hooks wants Burst and Speed. Glass Hour wants Wind and Mobility. False Face wants Speed and Wind.
- Bodies change on **chapter timescales**, not in a menu. Retraining is real and slow and narratively acknowledged.
- **Permanent injuries constrain further** — a ruined shoulder closes certain Custody nodes forever and redirects the player toward arts that do not need it.

**The result: a playthrough has a shape.** You will be a rooted pressure-grappler (Low River + Standing Water), or an outside deceiver (Split Reed + False Face), or a temple counter-fighter (Glass Hour + Low River), or something stranger. You cannot be all of them. **That is the fighting identity**, and it is produced by constraint rather than by a class selection screen.

**The Quiet partially dissolves the constraint** — not by giving you everything, but by letting your Instinctive set operate outside its own grammar. Which is the correct reward: it doesn't hand you the other styles, it frees the ones you actually earned.

### V1 style roster (scope decision)

| Style | V1 | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Low River** | ✅ Ship | The spine. Non-negotiable. |
| **Split Reed** | ✅ Ship | Required counter to Low River; without it the matchup web collapses into a ladder. |
| **Standing Water** | ✅ Ship | Owns Contact; hosts the richest Final Inch terminals; the moral core needs it. |
| **Glass Hour** | ✅ Ship | Thematically load-bearing (the Thousand Steps, the dying art, the final fight). Highest ceiling. |
| **The Quiet** | ✅ Ship | The endgame. The entire thesis. |
| **Hooks** | ⛔ Cut to post-launch | Its niche (pressure volume) is partly covered by aggressive Low River play. Cheapest cut. |
| **False Face** | ⛔ Cut to post-launch | Most expensive to build (it interacts with the AI Read system), and its best idea survives as the universal Lie, which improves base combat for every style. |

**4 styles + the Quiet in V1.** They cover all four distance bands and all three target resources. Recorded in `OPEN_DECISIONS.md` OD-05.

## 11. Style mastery — what levelling a style actually does

Per-style mastery has four states, and none of them are percentages:

| State | How it is reached | What it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Borrowed** | You have the stance and a handful of techniques. | You can fight in it, badly. Transitions are long. Signature mechanic operates at reduced effect. |
| **Held** | Sound mastery on the style's five core techniques. | Full signature mechanic. Normal transitions. |
| **Silent** | Silent mastery on five core techniques + a style trial (`TRAINING_AND_MASTERY.md` §6). | Shortened transitions, *flow variants* (transition inside a recovery), signature mechanic deepens. |
| **Instinctive** | Instinctive mastery on three core techniques + defeating a master of the style. | The style's late grammar unlocks (Low River's angled Line, Split Reed's forward measure, Standing Water's chained conversions, Glass Hour's pre-Borrow). Contributes toward the Quiet. |

Note that the final state requires **beating someone who owns the art**. You do not finish a style in a menu. You finish it in front of a person who spent their life on it, and in almost every case that person then has an opinion about you.
