# PROGRESSION.md
## Six axes of growth, and what is allowed to be a number

**The governing rule:** a thing may only be a number if the player would genuinely want to compare two of them. Everything else is content, state, or a discrete condition.

There is **no experience bar, no character level, no skill points, and no gear score anywhere in this game.**

---

## 1. The six axes

| Axis | Numeric? | Where it lives | How it advances |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Body** | Partly — three paired sliders under a hard budget | Diegetic: your silhouette | Conditioning over chapter timescales |
| **Technique** | No — five discrete states | The Ledger | Correct use under pressure |
| **Knowledge** | No — it is content | The Ledger | Acquisition, observation, study |
| **Reputation** | Hidden model, never shown | The world's behaviour | Witnessed patterns of conduct |
| **Relationships** | No — authored states | Dialogue and access | Time, conduct, and specific events |
| **Style mastery** | No — four states | The Ledger | Core-technique mastery + trials + beating a master |

---

## 2. Body

The physical character. Three **paired** attributes, each a slider between two genuinely useful opposites, under a **hard total budget** that grows only with age (and stops growing at around twenty-two).

| Pair | One end | Other end |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Root ↔ Mobility** | Immovable base, superb Structure retention, strong throws | Fast repositioning, longer evades, better angles |
| **Density ↔ Speed** | Absorbs impact, hits heavy, resists Breaks | Faster startup, more actions per exchange |
| **Wind ↔ Burst** | Deep Breath pool, slow recovery of committed actions | Shallow pool, explosive committed actions, fast recovery |

**Why pairs.** A slider you cannot max is a slider you must have an opinion about. There is no "best build" because there is no direction that is strictly better — and styles want opposite ends (`MARTIAL_STYLES.md` §10), so your body is a statement about who you intend to be.

**How it moves.** Conditioning is a **schedule decision**, not a grind. At each training opportunity you choose what to work, and the change accrues over in-game weeks. Moving a slider two notches toward Root means moving it two notches away from Mobility — slowly, visibly, and with dialogue from people who notice you have gotten heavier.

**Over-training injures.** Training the same attribute repeatedly without rest produces acute injuries. The schedule has a real cost and a real optimum, and the game never shows you the optimum.

**The body is visible.** The player's silhouette changes with their build (`ART_DIRECTION.md` §4, `ANIMATION_REQUIREMENTS.md` §3). A Root/Density fighter and a Mobility/Speed fighter are recognisably different people from across a courtyard. **Your build is your portrait.**

**What the body does NOT do:** it does not gate content, it does not multiply damage by a percentage, and it never appears as a number in the UI. It appears as three hand-drawn marks in the Ledger and as the shape of your own body on screen.

## 3. Technique — the mastery ladder

Five discrete states per technique. Full spec in `TRAINING_AND_MASTERY.md` §4.

**Impression → Learned → Sound → Silent → Instinctive**

What changes at each step is **startup, recovery, Breath cost, cancel windows, contextual variants, and the animation itself** — never a damage percentage. A Silent technique is faster because the motion is genuinely shorter.

**No percentages are ever shown.** The Ledger shows a technique's state as a word and, more usefully, shows *how it is drawn*: a technique at Impression is a scrawl with question marks; at Instinctive it is a clean, confident single line. The player reads their own mastery in their own handwriting.

---

## 4. Knowledge — the Ledger

Not a stat. A **physical notebook the character keeps**, which is also the game's entire menu system (`ART_DIRECTION.md` §6).

Contents:
- **Techniques**, each with its **provenance line**: where, when, from whom, and how acquired (taught / stolen / earned / derived / inherited).
- **Styles**, with mastery state and the player's own notes.
- **People**: everyone you have fought, with what you learned about them and what you did to them. This is the persistent-population record made legible.
- **Losses**: every significant defeat, with what beat you. Loss entries unlock specific training (`DEATH_AND_FAILURE` section of `TRAINING_AND_MASTERY.md` §8).
- **Derived counters**, which the player **names themselves**.

The Ledger is the answer to "a player should remember where important techniques came from." It is not a codex you can ignore; it is the pause menu.

## 5. Reputation

Fully specified in `MORALITY_AND_REPUTATION.md`. Summarised here only to place it on the progression map:

Reputation is a **hidden two-layer model** — objective Record (what happened, and who saw it) feeding subjective Reputation (what specific people and factions *believe*, assembled from rumours that travel with delay and distortion). It is never shown as a number, a bar, or a label. It progresses by witnessed pattern, weights costly acts far above free ones, and does not decay uniformly.

It gates access, changes opponent Will, alters boss encounters, and selects endings.

## 6. Relationships

Per-character authored states, not affinity meters.

Each significant NPC holds a **stance toward the player** drawn from a small authored set (for example: *curious / invested / wary / indebted / disappointed / afraid / devoted / finished with you*). Transitions between states are triggered by **specific events**, not by accumulated points — a relationship changes because of a thing that happened, and the player can usually name it.

Effects: dialogue, access, training availability, whether they will fight beside you, whether they will fight you, and in the case of rivals, their entire trajectory (`RIVALS.md`).

**Ruhn is special.** His channel is separate from every other system in the game (`MASTER.md` §7): he does not receive rumours. He knows what he has seen and what three trusted people tell him. This is why the ending is earned rather than tallied.

---

## 7. Style mastery

Four states — **Borrowed → Held → Silent → Instinctive** — specified in `MARTIAL_STYLES.md` §11. The final state requires defeating a master of that art, which means style completion is always a *relationship event* as well as a mechanical one.

## 8. What progression looks like across the campaign

A shape rather than a curve. Each chapter should produce one of each kind of growth:

| Chapter | Body | Technique | Knowledge | Standing | Relationship |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Prologue (9)** | None. You are a child. | Falling. | That fighting is a *trade* here. | First Record entry. | Ruhn chooses you. |
| **Ch.1 (12–15)** | Budget opens; first real choices. | Low River to Sound. | The Ledger begins. | Village-scale only. | Ruhn: teacher → invested. |
| **Ch.2 (16)** | First deliberate shaping. | First stolen technique. | Second style. The Cut. | First rumours travel. | Kem. First loss. |
| **Ch.3 (19)** | Peak athletic budget. | First Silent techniques. | Employment; compromise. | Regional reputation. | Yeo Ansa hardens. First betrayal. |
| **Ch.4 (22)** | Budget caps. Build commits. | Style to Silent mastery. | Ninebell; institutions; Ruhn's past. | Named in the registry. | Ise. Ruhn strained. |
| **Ch.5 (24)** | First permanent injury likely. | Glass Hour available. | The broken teachers. The truth. | Feared or sought. | Major loss. |
| **Ch.6 (26)** | Body is what it is. | First Instinctive techniques. | Your doctrine has students. | The world organises around you. | Kem arrives as consequence. |
| **Ch.7 (27)** | — | **The Quiet.** | — | Sealed. | Ruhn. |

**Note the shape:** body growth front-loads and stops. Technique growth accelerates throughout. Knowledge broadens then deepens. Standing starts local and becomes structural. Relationships resolve. This is a life, not a curve.

## 9. Equipment

Deliberately minor (Pillar 6).

| Item | Effect | Power axis? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hand wraps** | Reduce accumulated hand injury from striking hard targets. | No |
| **Footwear** | Changes footing on wet stone, gravel, deck, ice. Iron-shod is stable and **loud** (affects stealth and the audio identity of your approach). | No |
| **Coat** | Warmth in the high country. Slightly slows rotation when heavy. **Persistent across the whole game** — it tears, is patched, and is repaired in the local style of wherever it was repaired. Zero mechanical benefit beyond warmth. | No |
| **Bindings / braces** | Manage an acute injury at the cost of mobility in that limb. | No |

There is no rarity, no crafting, no durability minigame, no vendor economy of power. **Money exists** (you are paid for escort work and pit fights) and it buys food, lodging, travel, medicine, training from paid teachers, and obligations discharged — never power.

## 10. The anti-spreadsheet checklist

Applied to every progression proposal:

1. Does it show the player a number they will optimise instead of playing? → Cut.
2. Does it make a technique *better* rather than *different*? → Suspect. Prefer new options.
3. Could the player understand their progress with the menu closed? → Must be yes.
4. Does it reward time spent rather than understanding gained? → Cut.
5. Does it let the player buy a solution they did not earn in a fight? → Cut.
6. Would a spreadsheet make this game easier to play well? → If yes, the design is wrong.
