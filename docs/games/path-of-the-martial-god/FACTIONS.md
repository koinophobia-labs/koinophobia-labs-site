# FACTIONS.md
## The five powers of the Concord

**The governing rule: there is no good school and no evil school.** Each faction is an **institution with an economic function**, a coherent worldview that is defensible, an internal split that is tearing it apart, and a specific grievance with Ruhn. None of them is the villain. All of them will use the player if allowed.

**The unifying question the factions exist to ask:** when the state collapses and violence becomes infrastructure, *who is allowed to hit people, and who decides?* Each faction is an answer. All five answers are bad. That is the point.

---

## 1. The Ninth Bell — the arbitration houses

> *"A blow struck outside the record is just a crime with a stance."*

- **Function:** settles civil disputes by proxy duel, under written rules, with binding results. The last surviving organ of the Concordance. Genuine legal authority in the cities, none at all in the Cut.
- **Worldview:** violence is inevitable, therefore violence must be a **court**. Rules, witnesses, registrars, appeals. A duel that produces a document is civilisation; a duel that produces only a winner is barbarism. They are not wrong.
- **Signature discipline:** *Bell Form* — a formal, rule-bound derivative of Low River adapted for scored duels: no strikes to the throat or spine, points for structural dominance, a mandated salute. It is technically excellent and strategically hollow, because it has been optimised for a ruleset rather than for a fight. Ninth Bell fighters are famously hard to beat inside the lines and brittle outside them.
- **Visual identity:** deep green and brass. Long coats with a registrar's clasp. Ceremonial batons carried but almost never used. Immaculate, slightly out of date, visibly expensive to maintain.
- **Leadership:** an **Arbiter Council** of eleven, chaired by **Ise Vau-ran's** grandmother, **Ise Ha-rin**, who is ninety-one, entirely lucid, and the most dangerous political mind in the game.
- **Internal conflict:** **the rules are being bought.** Contract holders now retain arbitration houses on permanent fee, and a retained house tends to find for its client. Half the Council knows and considers it survival; half considers it the end. Ise Vauran is in the second half and is losing.
- **Relationship with Ruhn:** he *was* the Ninth Bell — its finest fighter, undefeated for eleven years, and then the instrument of the thing that broke it. Officially, Ruhn Ijae-so is a corrected entry in the registry. Unofficially, every senior arbiter knows exactly what he did and that they ordered it.
- **Relationship with the player:** they want you **licensed**. A registered, answerable martial god is a tool; an unregistered one is a crisis. Their offer is real and their protection is real, and the price is that you belong to the record.

## 2. The Iron Road — the escort houses and rail guard

> *"A technique that does not work in a freight car is decoration."*

- **Function:** cargo security along the rail and river routes. The largest employer of fighters in the Concord and the single biggest reason martial skill has a market price.
- **Worldview:** ruthlessly practical. There is no philosophy, only outcomes, and the outcome is whether the shipment arrived. They are the reason the arts survived the collapse at all — they paid for them — and the reason the arts are dying, because they only pay for the parts that work fast.
- **Signature discipline:** *the Compact* — a stripped, teachable, six-month version of Standing Water plus a dozen dirty strikes. Designed to be learned by an ordinary adult in half a year and to work in a confined space against someone holding a knife. Contemptible to purists. Extremely effective.
- **Visual identity:** rust, canvas, oiled leather, iron-shod boots. Company badges. Scarves against coal smoke. They look like workers because they are.
- **Leadership:** **Tor Beshan**, a former escort who now sits in an office in Ironmouth, and above him a board of contract holders who have never been in a fight. Tor knows he is the last fighter who will ever run this company.
- **Internal conflict:** **the founders were fighters; the owners are shareholders.** The old escorts believe the house owes its people protection and a pension; the board believes it owes its clients a delivery. When a crew is lost, the argument becomes concrete and ugly.
- **Relationship with Ruhn:** he broke four of their founding instructors. They have never forgiven it and have also, quietly, benefited more than anyone — every teacher he crippled was a teacher of a fifteen-year art that the Compact replaced.
- **Relationship with the player:** they want to **employ** you. The work is honest, well paid, morally compromising in small increments, and it is where the player learns that being good at violence makes you useful to people whose goals are not yours. **Chapter 3 lives here.**

## 3. The Thousand Steps — the high temples

> *"We are not preserving it. We are watching it die and refusing to look away."*

- **Function:** none, economically. That is their crisis. They teach the pre-Concordance arts as disciplines rather than trades, to almost nobody.
- **Worldview:** the art is an end in itself. A technique's value is not what it accomplishes but what learning it does to the person. Fifteen years to competence, and the fifteen years are the point. They are also — and the game is honest about this — **proud, insular, and complicit in their own extinction.**
- **Signature discipline:** **Glass Hour** (`MARTIAL_STYLES.md` §6). The highest ceiling in the world and the least employable art in existence.
- **Visual identity:** fog-white, slate grey, one vermilion cord. Undyed wool, bare feet on cold stone, breath visible. Utterly plain and instantly recognisable.
- **Leadership:** **Nas Il-ke**, a woman of about seventy who is the last person alive who can teach Glass Hour to Instinctive, and who has refused every student for nine years. Her reason is not pride. It is that the last student she trained to that level went down the mountain and took escort work.
- **Internal conflict:** **adapt or die, and both are losing.** A younger faction wants to open the art — shorten it, teach it, take Iron Road money, survive. Nas Il-ke believes an art you can learn in six months is not the art. Both sides know the other is right.
- **Relationship with Ruhn:** **he broke their generation.** Of the eleven teachers who could have carried Glass Hour forward, Ruhn crippled seven. Nas Il-ke was the eighth; he beat her and did not break her, and she has never understood why, and the not-understanding has taken fifty years of her life. She is the only person alive who Ruhn still listens to, and one of the three people who can tell him about you (`MASTER.md` §7).
- **Relationship with the player:** they will teach you **only if the world's report of you does not reach them first as butchery**, and they judge on what they see. They are the hardest door in the game to open and the most valuable room behind it.

## 4. The Cut — the pit circuit

> *"The art belongs to whoever bleeds for it. Everything else is paperwork."*

- **Function:** the underground fight economy of the mining towns. Ticketed bouts, side betting, company scrip, and the only route out of the seam for a person with no papers.
- **Worldview:** not evil — **desperate**, and clear-eyed about it. The Cut believes the registered classes stole the arts and put a fee on them, and the Cut is basically right. It is the most vital, generous, and dangerous community in the game. People here will share food with you and then try to end your career for a week's wages, and see no contradiction.
- **Signature discipline:** **False Face** (`MARTIAL_STYLES.md` §8), plus whatever works. The Cut has the best raw fighters in the Concord and the worst habits.
- **Visual identity:** coal-black and sodium-orange. Bare hands, wrapped knuckles, borrowed boots, company scrip in the pocket. Faces marked. The crowd is part of the identity — the Cut's chalkboard of use-names is the closest thing the Concord has to a ranking, and it is maintained meticulously by people who cannot read.
- **Leadership:** no leaders — **bookers**. **Oru Khen** books Ashgate, takes forty percent, and is the closest thing the game has to a straightforwardly exploitative man. But he is not the faction; the fighters are, and they know exactly what he is.
- **Internal conflict:** **the fighters versus the men who book the fights.** A recurring, unwinnable labour struggle. Every few years someone tries to organise the circuit and every few years it fails, usually because a booker pays a fighter to make it fail.
- **Relationship with Ruhn:** **they do not know who he is,** which is the only place in the world that is true, and it is why he lives near enough to visit. The Cut has a legend about the Ninebell Butcher and tells it wrong.
- **Relationship with the player:** the Cut is where **reputation is made and distorted** (`WORLD.md` §7). It exaggerates on purpose, because a frightening fighter sells tickets. The player's use-name is probably invented here, and they do not get to choose it.

## 5. The Vessel — the new school

> *"Lineage is just a dead man's opinion with a building."*

- **Function:** a reform movement turned school, twenty years old, synthesising techniques across traditions without anyone's permission. Growing fast. Winning a lot.
- **Worldview:** the old arts were optimised for a world that no longer exists; the correct response is to take everything that works and discard the ceremony. They are **right about a great deal** and have no idea what they are throwing away — the fifteen-year disciplines produced people, not just fighters, and the Vessel's graduates are superb, shallow, and interchangeable.
- **Signature discipline:** **Hooks** (`MARTIAL_STYLES.md` §7), registered as Third Form, Vessel Lineage, which is an irony their founder enjoys.
- **Visual identity:** modern, plain, cheap, and deliberately without insignia — dark work clothes, taped hands, no colours. They look like a gym, because they are one. The absence of identity *is* the identity.
- **Leadership:** **Dul Ka-sen**, thirty-four, a genuine synthesising genius with no patience, no manners, and no interest in whether anyone approves. Beat three Ninth Bell champions in a single year to prove a point, and then refused the licence.
- **Internal conflict:** **success is turning them into what they replaced.** The Vessel now has a building, a fee schedule, a curriculum, and a waiting list. Dul Ka-sen can feel it happening and does not know how to stop it without destroying the thing he built.
- **Relationship with Ruhn:** Dul Ka-sen has worked out who Ruhn is — the only person in the game who figures it out independently — and regards him not as a monster but as **the last complete proof of what a real lineage produces.** He wants to meet him. Ruhn will not.
- **Relationship with the player:** the Vessel is where the player's own **synthesis is culturally legible**. Everyone else sees a person betraying a tradition; the Vessel sees a person doing the obvious thing. They are the faction most likely to *celebrate* the player, which is its own kind of trap.

## 6. Faction interaction rules

1. **No faction meters.** Each faction holds a **Standing belief** assembled from rumours it has received (`MORALITY_AND_REPUTATION.md`), never a number shown to the player.
2. **Factions are not exclusive, but they are jealous.** You can work for the Iron Road and train at the Thousand Steps. Both will find out, and each will draw a conclusion about you that is *partly accurate*.
3. **Every faction's door can close permanently**, and never with a warning.
4. **Every faction wants the player for the same reason** — you are becoming the strongest argument in any room, and each of them has an argument they would like to win. Only one person in the game wants nothing from you, and that is the whole tragedy of `MASTER.md`.
5. **No faction is completable.** There is no reputation track to fill, no rank ladder to climb, no faction ending. They are institutions; you pass through them.

## 7. What each faction would do with a martial god

The question that structures the final act. Each faction's answer is sincere:

| Faction | What they'd do with you | Why it's reasonable | Why it's a catastrophe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ninth Bell** | License and constrain you. Make you answerable. | An unanswerable person is a legal void. | A licensed god is a state monopoly on violence with no state behind it. |
| **Iron Road** | Employ you. Put you on the most valuable route. | You'd save lives. Genuinely. | Your conduct becomes a commercial asset, priced and sold. |
| **Thousand Steps** | Have you inherit the art and teach it. | The art survives. | They'd be handing an unanswerable weapon a lineage — exactly what produced Ruhn. |
| **The Cut** | Make you a symbol. | You'd be theirs, and they have nothing. | Symbols get people killed, and the Cut cannot control what it worships. |
| **The Vessel** | Study you. Codify you. Teach you to hundreds. | The art advances. | A hundred shallow copies of you, with your habits and none of your reasons. This is **exactly what Kem becomes** (`RIVALS.md` §2). |

Ruhn's answer is the sixth option, and it is the one the game ends on.
