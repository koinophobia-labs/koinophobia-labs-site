# CHARACTERS.md
## The protagonist, the life arc, and the supporting cast

See `MASTER.md` for Ruhn and `RIVALS.md` for Yeo Ansa, Kem Duro, Ise Vauran, and Nas Orin.

---

## 1. The protagonist

### Design constraints
- **No prophecy, no bloodline, no innate gift.** The player is chosen for a pause, not for talent (`MASTER.md` §6). Any line of dialogue implying destiny is a bug.
- **Fully voiced, with a fixed name and a selectable presentation.** The character is **Vahn** — a name that works across presentations, and a person with a fixed history, because an unvoiced blank protagonist cannot carry a relationship with Ruhn, and that relationship is the game.
- **Player-selectable body presentation and voice** at the prologue, with no mechanical difference. The Body system (`PROGRESSION.md` §2) supplies all physical variation, and it is earned rather than chosen.
- **Vahn speaks little.** They were raised by a man who does not speak. Their dialogue is short, and the game's emotional work is done by what they *do* at speed.

### Who Vahn is
A river-village child from Mudgate with no particular quality except one: they stop. Not out of goodness — at nine they could not tell you why. They just could not do it.

Over eighteen years the game asks whether that survives being taught to be dangerous, and it asks in the only way that counts: at speed, under pressure, with nobody watching but a man on a levee who never says anything.

### What is authored and what is the player's
| Authored | The player's |
| --- | --- |
| Vahn's history, family, and village | Every Final Inch |
| The people they meet and when | Their fighting identity and body |
| Ruhn's decisions | How they treat the defeated |
| The major losses | What they do with Nas Orin |
| That they will become dangerous | What being dangerous turns them into |

### Family
- **A mother, Vahn Sae-ri**, a net-mender and the reason Ruhn is tolerated in the village. Alive through Chapter 4. Never a hostage, never fridged, never leverage. She thinks the whole thing is faintly ridiculous and worries about the player's hands.
- **A younger brother, Vahn Do-ken**, six years younger. Not a fighter, and notably uninterested. He becomes a telegraph clerk at Ironmouth, which means that by Chapter 5 **the player's own brother is one of the nodes through which their reputation travels** (`WORLD.md` §7) — and he has to decide what to transmit about his sibling. One of the best small scenes in the game.
- **No father.** Not dead-and-avenged; simply gone downriver years ago, unremarkable, unmourned, and never a plot point. The absence is the reason a nine-year-old accepts an old man's offer without questioning it, and it is never discussed.

## 2. The life arc

Four phases across eighteen years. **Time passing is a mechanic, not a transition** — the world ages, people die, institutions change, and the player's own body becomes a record of what they chose.

### Phase I — The Child (age 9, Prologue, ~60–90 min)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Story purpose** | Establish the world's economy of violence, the pause, and Ruhn |
| **Physical capability** | Small, weak, fast-recovering. Cannot bear weight, cannot break structure, cannot hurt an adult. |
| **Available styles** | None. Flailing, and then falling. |
| **Emotional state** | Unformed. Not afraid of much, because nothing has happened yet. |
| **Relationship to Ruhn** | A stranger on a levee |
| **World access** | One village |
| **Mechanics introduced** | Movement, guard, the Structure base, **falling**, **the Final Inch** |
| **What visibly changes about combat** | Nothing yet — this is the baseline the whole game is measured against |

**The design point of the child phase:** the player's first experience of combat is **losing**, and the tutorial is *how to be defeated safely*. A player who has felt incompetence for ninety minutes will feel competence for the next twenty-five hours.

### Phase II — The Student (ages 12–16, Chapters 1–2, ~6 hours)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Story purpose** | The relationship. The art. The first departure. |
| **Physical capability** | Gangly and uneven. **Reach changes faster than control** — a deliberate, slightly frustrating period where the character's own growing body is an obstacle, expressed in animation and in altered technique ranges. Strength arrives before judgement. |
| **Available styles** | Low River (Learned→Sound). A stolen Split Reed stance carried badly. |
| **Emotional state** | Devoted, impatient, and certain that the training is slower than it needs to be. |
| **Relationship to Ruhn** | Total dependence, then the first real anger — he sends you away *before you are ready*, on purpose. |
| **World access** | Mudgate, the Long Water, the Cut |
| **Mechanics introduced** | The Ledger, style switching, Breath economy, stolen techniques, the Read (first exposure — a pit fighter starts countering your habit) |
| **What visibly changes** | **Rough → Sound animation tier.** The single clearest progression beat in the game. |

### Phase III — The Professional (ages 19–24, Chapters 3–5, ~11 hours)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Story purpose** | Compromise, institutions, the truth about Ruhn, and the major loss |
| **Physical capability** | Peak athleticism, worst judgement. The body can do anything and the person has not decided what any of it is for. **The first permanent injury lands here** and it is designed as character, not punishment (`COMBAT_SYSTEM.md` §12). |
| **Available styles** | Two to three at Held or Silent. Glass Hour becomes available at the end of the phase, if the door opens. |
| **Emotional state** | Competent, useful, and being used. The first time the player is genuinely good at something and discovers that being good at it makes them valuable to people whose goals are not theirs. |
| **Relationship to Ruhn** | Distant, performative, then cracked open by the registry and by Nas Il-ke |
| **World access** | The whole Concord |
| **Mechanics introduced** | Silent tier, martial trials, the rumour network becoming visible, persistent injury, **Nas Orin** |
| **What visibly changes** | **Sound → Silent.** Excess motion disappears. The player becomes quiet. |

### Phase IV — The Answer (ages 26–27, Chapters 6–7, ~6 hours)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Story purpose** | Consequence arrives, and then the argument |
| **Physical capability** | Complete and marked. The body is what the player made it, permanently, with whatever it lost along the way. |
| **Available styles** | Instinctive techniques, and **the Quiet** |
| **Emotional state** | The world has begun to organise around them, and they can feel it |
| **Relationship to Ruhn** | He is afraid of you |
| **World access** | Everywhere, and doors closing |
| **Mechanics introduced** | The Quiet, Accord, Instinctive terminals |
| **What visibly changes** | The player's idle stance becomes Ruhn's idle stance, and nobody points it out |

### How the passage of time is expressed
1. **The village changes.** Mudgate is visited in the Prologue, Ch.2, Ch.6, and Ch.7. Buildings go up, the rope bridge is replaced with an iron one, people die, the shrine is finally repaired by someone.
2. **Characters age on the same clock.** Ruhn goes from 68 to 86. Nas Il-ke dies. Ise Ha-rin does not, which is its own joke.
3. **The technology moves.** Oil lamps become arc lights in Ironmouth between Ch.3 and Ch.6. The telegraph reaches Ashgate, which changes how fast your reputation travels — a *mechanical* consequence of the world's progress.
4. **The same drill, eighteen years apart.** Ruhn and the player do the **Hands** drill in Chapter 1 and again in the final chapter. Same camera, same yard, same two people, everything else different. This is the cheapest and most devastating thing in the game.

## 3. Supporting cast

### Nas Il-ke — the Thousand Steps
Seventy at first meeting, the last person alive who can teach Glass Hour to Instinctive, and she has refused every student for nine years. Ruhn beat her fifty years ago **and did not break her**, and she has never understood why, and the not-understanding has occupied her life.

She is the one who tells the player who Ruhn is. She does it without malice, over tea, as though it were common knowledge, and it is the worst scene in the game in the best way.

She is the second of Ruhn's three trusted informants. She dies between Chapters 6 and 7 — offscreen, of age, having finally taken a student. Whether the student is the player is a function of the player's conduct.

### Han Su-ye — the registrar
A Ninth Bell clerk in her sixties who has maintained Ruhn's corrected file for forty years and never told anyone. Bureaucratically unremarkable, personally unmovable. She is how the player learns their teacher has a file, and she decides — based on the player's conduct in Ninebell — how much of it to show them.

The third of Ruhn's informants, and the only one who reports to him **by letter**, which means her reports arrive weeks late and are the reason his model of the player is sometimes out of date in ways that matter.

### Dul Ka-sen — the Vessel
Thirty-four, brilliant, impatient, contemptuous of lineage, and the only person who independently works out who Ruhn is. He wants to meet him. He is not permitted to. He is the loudest advocate for the idea that the player should be *studied and reproduced*, and he means it as a compliment.

### Tor Beshan — the Iron Road
The last fighter who will ever run the escort company, and he knows it. Gives the player their first honest wage and their first genuine moral compromise in the same week, and is decent about both. He is the adult version of the trap: a good man administering a system that does bad things at a rate he has decided to tolerate.

### Oru Khen — the booker
Takes forty percent at Ashgate. The most straightforwardly exploitative person in the game and, notably, **not a fighter** — the player can destroy him at any time and it would accomplish nothing, which several characters point out.

### Ise Ha-rin — Arbiter Council chair
Ninety-one, entirely lucid, the most dangerous political mind in the Concord, and the only character who is never afraid of the player for a second. She ordered the thing Ruhn did. She would do it again. She says so.

### The dogs
There are two. They are in the Cut, they belong to nobody, they are extremely dangerous, and **they cannot be lied to** (`MARTIAL_STYLES.md` §8). They are also the only opponents in the game the player cannot take a Final Inch against, because the game does not offer it. Nobody comments on this.

## 4. Casting and performance requirements

| Role | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| **Ruhn** | First-choice performer, full performance capture, non-negotiable budget line. The role is 80% silence; cast for presence, not for voice. |
| **Vahn** | Two performers (presentation options), full capture, **including a child performer for the prologue and a second for the adolescent phase** — four voices total across the life arc, matched for continuity. |
| **Yeo Ansa, Kem, Ise Vauran** | Full capture. Kem's performance must carry warmth in Chapter 2 and the *same* warmth, unchanged, in Chapter 6, when it has become horrifying. |
| **Everyone else** | Voice only, with body from the general animation pool. |

**Aging:** Ruhn ages 18 years on screen. Handled with a single scanned identity plus authored age states rather than four separate captures (`ANIMATION_REQUIREMENTS.md` §3).
